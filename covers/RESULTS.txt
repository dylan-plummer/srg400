Twelve Hoffman–Singleton covers and a reversible construction on 400 vertices
25 November 2026

1. Results

For the fixed simultaneous translation group C5 x C5 on the biaffine
Hoffman–Singleton graph, there are exactly 80 labeled decompositions of
its complement B into triangular graphs T(5). Under arbitrary isomorphism
there are twelve classes. Number 0 recovers the srg400 paper. Classes 1
through 11 are additional. These are counts for this prescribed translation
action. Other decompositions of B may exist.

Each of the twelve covers yields two graph families, one with parameters
(400,168,68,72) and one with parameters (400,171,74,72). We give a bijection
between their ordinary isomorphism classes which preserves the entire
automorphism group. It is defined intrinsically on the graphs and works for
every decoration of every cover in the catalogue. Summing over the twelve
disjoint families gives the following exact count at EACH degree:

Every family member at degree 171 differs from both archived Kovaľ examples.
Those have respectively 3,740 and 21,660 vanishing four-row sums of their
binary closed-neighborhood matrices. Our number is 700 throughout. The
two archived examples have full group orders 3,840 and 5,760. The three
archived graph6 files checked include a canonical copy of the second graph.
The degree-168 row was realized by the earlier srg400 construction. The
degree-171 row is credited to Kovaľ in Brouwer's table. This is a family
and transfer result on those two rows.

2. Three ten-tuples suffice

Label the fifty points by 25s+5x+y, for s in {0,1} and x,y in F5.
The Hoffman–Singleton graph has edges within a fixed (s,x) at y-differences
+/-1 on sheet 0 and +/-2 on sheet 1. A point (0,x,y) is adjacent to
(1,u,v) when v-y=-3(u-x)^2. Take the complement B.

A ten-tuple lists base points in the lexicographic order of the ten edges
of K5 on 0,...,4. Two entries represent an edge of the tile exactly when
their K5 edges meet. For example, cover 10 has starters

     0 15  8 24  6 21 14 12  2 18
     0 48 49  3  2 45 46  4 47  1
    25 33 39 43 19 24 48 47  6 13

Simultaneously translate x,y on both sheets. The three orbits have lengths
5,5,25. Their 35 tiles partition all 1,050 edges of B. In covers 10 and 11
thirty of the 35 supports are independent ten-sets in the Hoffman–Singleton
graph. The other five induce fifteen edges each. In the original cover
exactly 25 supports induced three edges, five induced five edges and five
induced fifteen. The contrast can be seen before signing a single edge.

For a tile let D be an oriented vertex-edge incidence matrix of K5 and put
X=2I-D^t D. Its nonzero off-diagonal entries are precisely the tile edges.
It has eigenvalues 2 and -3, of multiplicities 6 and 4. Replace each base
point p by F2^3. Assign its seven nontrivial characters bijectively to the
seven tiles containing p. Each assigned character may have an arbitrary
sign, independently at every incident point-tile flag. For pq in tile T,
join (p,x) and (q,y) when chi_pT(x)chi_qT(y)=X[p,q]. Fibers are independent.
On fiber constants the adjacency is 4B. On the characters assigned to a
tile it acts as 4X, up to conjugation by signs. These subspaces exhaust
all 400 dimensions. The resulting degree and restricted eigenvalues are
168,8,-12, giving lambda=68 and mu=72. This lift and the initial cover
are from the earlier srg400 work, which gives credit to Kovaľ's search
ledger for the base, slab and signed T(5) framework.

3. The second degree

Every cover has a unique collection P of five tiles whose ten-point
supports partition the base. This was checked by exact cover rooted at
the first uncovered point for all twelve, independently also on the graphs
reconstructed from graph6. Keep X on these tiles and replace X by -X on
the thirty other tiles. In the fiber of p, use its unique tile in P to
split its eight vertices by that character into two K4s. A change to the
sign of the character interchanges the two halves and gives the same edges.

The constant block becomes 4B+3I, of eigenvalues 171,11,-9. The internal
degree-three adjacency acts by 3 on the distinguished character and by -1
on each other nonconstant character. Thus the remaining blocks are
4X+3I (tiles in P) and -4X-I (all other tiles). Each has roots 11 and -9.
This proves A^2=99I+2A+72J, hence parameters (400,171,74,72). Equivalently,
complement each external eight-by-eight block belonging to the thirty tiles
and add the internal cliques. Reversing both operations recovers degree 168.

4. Why different covers give different ordinary graphs

For degree 168 take binary rows of A, and for degree 171 take rows of A+I.
At each target fiber the dependence on the target coordinate is affine
linear, with slope given by the appropriate port character. For a row in
the target fiber itself the closed-neighborhood convention supplies exactly
the distinguished character at degree 171. At degree 168 its slope is zero.

If four rows sum to zero, consider the base points occurring an odd number
of times. If there are two or four of them, a target fiber exists where the
symmetric difference of their port sets has size one or two. A nonzero
character and a sum of two distinct nonzero characters both have nonzero
slope. This test uses only the unsigned ports and was run over all 1,225
pairs and all 230,300 four-subsets on each of the twelve covers, at each
degree. If the pattern is two plus two, the difference within one fiber
gives a nonzero constant on the base support of exactly four of its seven
ports. The 50 times binomial(7,4) such supports are all distinct. Two pairs
in different fibers therefore cannot cancel. Within one fiber the four-row
sum vanishes exactly when the four distinct affine points sum to zero.

This gives exactly 14 affine planes per fiber, or 700 in all. Joining
vertices in these four-sets recovers the fifty fibers as connected
components. At a fixed fiber, half-blocks give the seven distinct character
partitions. Join two incident base edges when they use the same partition.
The resulting components recover the triangular tiles. In particular the
entire cover and its unique P are intrinsic. The forward and reverse
operations in section 3 commute with every isomorphism.

5. Enumeration and counts

The simultaneous translation group H has order 25 and acts freely on
points and on host edges, giving 42 differences. A ten-point support
cannot be fixed by H. Since the number of tiles is 35, their H-orbit
lengths are either 5+5+25 or seven occurrences of 5. A short tile has
a stabilizer of order five which acts faithfully on its five K5 symbols.
After naming the symbols cyclically, its ten edges occur in two cycles
(i,i+1) and (i,i+2). Their point images are x+iv and y+iv for a nonzero
v in F5^2, with arbitrary choices of sheets and relative displacement.
Each short orbit covers six distinct host differences, with multiplicity
five in a single tile. Enumeration gives 235 short orbits, 175 difference
masks, and zero seven-mask partitions. A long tile must hit each of the
thirty remaining differences exactly once. The possible assignments of its
ten K5 edges to sheets were exhaustively reduced under the five symbols.

The primary backtracker took 372 problems modulo host symmetries. An
independent implementation ran all 7,260 problems for all 4,080 remaining
masks, with no host symmetry reduction, and constructed all 80 labeled
covers. It found ten labeled covers in each class of group order 50 and
five in each class of order 100. Canonicalization used both a 260-vertex
point/tile/star incidence graph and a 1,135-vertex point/tile/edge incidence
graph. The latter was also reconstructed from ordinary graph6 data and
checked using bliss, including a randomly permuted input.

For each fiber the seven-bit character value vectors form one of 30 binary
simplex codes, with 16 possible affine cosets per code. These 480 local
states classify all assignments up to fiber relabelling. A simultaneous
sign flip on all ten flags of a tile gives the same graph. No nonzero
combination of tile flips preserves even one assignment: any such set of
tiles would meet each point in zero or four flags, and exhaustive testing
of the even point-tile incidence kernel leaves only the empty set. Thus
before dividing by cover automorphisms the size is 480^50 / 2^35.
The earlier Burnside algorithm for the signed action on these local states
was applied to every conjugacy class of each of the twelve cover groups.
Its fixed counts and the complete per-cover answers are in counts.json.
The leading elementary lower bound is independently given by orbit size,
(4/50+8/100) times 480^50 / 2^35. The count is for all decorations of the
specified unsigned covers.

There are 117,376 graph classes at each degree admitting the chosen
translations. Four covers have group C5 x D10, with |D10|=10. The other
eight have group (C5 x C5) semidirect C4, acting on F5^2 with eigenvalues
-1 and 2 for a suitable generator. Each such cover yields 32 classes at
each degree with this full group, for 256 in total. The point orbits of all
cover groups have sizes 25 and 25. No member is vertex-transitive. The
translations form the unique Sylow 5-subgroup of each cover group. The
faithfulness on the cover follows from the free tile action above.

6. Geometry

Within a tile a negative signed triangle excludes its three projected
points from any clique. Enumerate subsets of the base which are cliques
in B and avoid every such triple. For degree 168 each lifted clique takes
at most one point per fiber. Every cover has universal clique bound 10,
excluding the 15-point lines of a pg(14,11,6).

At degree 171, a clique's points in a fiber lie in the half-space for the
distinguished tile and in one half-space for every distinct external port
used by the projection. If j distinct nonzero characters are involved,
their rank is at least ceil(log2(j+1)). The contribution of the fiber
is at most 8 / 2^ceil(log2(j+1)). Summing and taking the maximum over
the same projected-clique enumeration, using the new tile signs, gives
upper bound 15 on covers 4,5 and 12 on the other ten. In particular none
of these companions has a 20-clique. The OA(20,9) geometry suggested in
Brouwer's table requires such cliques. This also rules out subtracting a
20-clique spread from any of these companions to construct degree 152.

7. Reproduction

The graph on cover 10 with full group of order 100 can be rebuilt with
the standard library, using this directory's JSON data and the command

    python from_starters.py 10 168 --symmetric > example.g6

Replace 168 by 171 for its companion. graphs/ contains 48 ordinary
graph6 files, a translation-invariant pair and a maximally symmetric
pair per cover. All 48 have been rebuilt byte for byte independently
from the ten-tuples. audit_graphs.py reads graphs without any starter or
port data, verifies all 79,800 unordered pairs per graph, reconstructs
the fibers and covers and checks both directions of the companion map.
It also checks all archived Kovaľ comparison files. See audit.tsv.

With Python from the srg environment, run census_check.py for the second
complete search (it compiles census_check.cpp using g++ -std=c++20).
Reproduce the primary census using short_orbits.py, problems.py, then
compile all_long.cpp with C++20, feed it problems.in and save stdout to
embeddings.txt. Run catalogue.py, companions.py, equivariant.py, groups.py,
geometry.py and audit_graphs.py. The primary programs also use the earlier
work/srg400_construction_20261124 and work/srg400_family_20261124 code.
The independent census and from_starters.py use no earlier campaign code.
